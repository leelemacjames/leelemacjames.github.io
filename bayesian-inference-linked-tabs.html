Estimation and evidence

Two linked Bayesian exemplars: posterior estimation through the classical circle-in-a-square construction, and theory-informed hypothesis comparison through a Bayes factor.

One Bayesian framework, two different questions

Both builds combine assumptions with a likelihood supplied by observed data. They differ in the inferential object: the first estimates an unknown parameter; the second compares the predictive adequacy of two hypotheses.

Posterior estimation

What values remain plausible?

Random points are sampled from a square. Whether each point lies inside its inscribed circle is a Bernoulli observation with probability q = π/4. A beta posterior over q therefore induces a posterior over π.

Result: posterior density, posterior mean and credible interval.

Model comparison

Which hypothesis predicts the data better?

The Bayes-factor build compares the point null θ = 0 with a distribution of theoretically predicted effects under H1. Its answer depends on both the data and the specified model of H1.

Result: BF₁₀, with BF₀₁ as its reciprocal.
