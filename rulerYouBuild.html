The ruler you build,
and the one you drop

Three constants will make you a length, a mass and a time. This walkthrough asks you to build those standards yourself, then to find out what a fourth constant does to them, and finally to ask which kinds of matter have ever been made to agree.

Mmass: how much resists acceleration, and how much responds to gravity
Llength: what a ruler compares, whether the ruler is metal or atomic
Ttime: what a clock compares, whether the clock is a pendulum or a transition

1Constants are exchange rates

What the square brackets keep, and what they throw away

Writing [v] = LT-1 throws away the numerical unit and keeps the kind of quantity, so metres per second and miles per hour become the same dimensional object. A dimensionally homogeneous equation therefore survives the replacement of metres by feet, which is a severe constraint on what an equation may say, though it says nothing about which ingredients nature actually uses.

Read each constant as a rate of exchange rather than as a number to look up. The speed of light buys a length with a time; Planck's constant buys an energy with a frequency; the gravitational constant tells a mass how strongly it takes part in the gravitational relation.

[ħ] = M L2 T-1    [c] = L T-1    [G] = M-1 L3 T-2    [H] = T-1    [Λ] = L-2

Worked example: the pendulum

Assume the period depends only on the length and on the local gravitational acceleration, so that T = κ ℓa gb. Matching powers of L and T gives a = 1/2 and b = -1/2, hence T = κ√(ℓ/g). The units have recovered the shape of the law and left κ = 2π for geometry to supply.

The pendulum result looks like a derivation. Say precisely which two things it assumed before any algebra took place, and which thing it could never have produced.

It assumed, first, that the period depends on ℓ and g alone, excluding amplitude and mass, and second, that the dependence is a product of powers rather than any other function of the ingredients. Physics chose the ingredients and dimensional analysis policed their combination.

It could never produce the dimensionless factor 2π, because dimensionless numbers are invisible to a method that only tracks M, L and T. The same blindness will matter a great deal in section 3.

With ħ, c and G the answer is forced. Each of relativity, quantum theory and gravitation has contributed one independent dimensional direction, and together they span the space, so exactly one monomial length exists. That uniqueness is a fact about the ingredient list rather than a discovery about nature, and it does not establish that the Planck length is a smallest length.

Uniqueness follows from the count. Three ingredients spanning three independent dimensions leave no freedom, so the exponents are determined by linear algebra alone. Change the ingredient list and the unique answer changes with it, which shows that the authority belongs to the list rather than to the world.

The two masses you built differ by roughly sixty orders of magnitude, and nothing in the algebra prefers either one.

3The fourth constant

Where the menu becomes a continuum

Now select all four of ħ, c, G and H, and ask for a length. The matrix still has rank three, because the ingredients still span only M, L and T, so one combination of them escapes the dimensions altogether. Drag the slider that appears and watch a dimensionally impeccable length travel from far below a proton to far beyond the observable universe.

Ξ = ħ G H2c5 = (tP / tH)2   ≈   10-122   today

Since Ξ carries no dimensions, multiplying any answer by Ξs leaves its dimensions untouched for every real s. Choosing three constants out of four does not avoid this; it silently sets one exponent to zero and presents the result as though it were the only option. A table of four such triples is a set of convenient representatives drawn from a continuum.

This is the same blindness that hid 2π in section 1, arriving now at a scale where it hides a factor of 10122.

A colleague notices that ħ, G and H give a length of about sixty metres and calls it suggestive. Using the slider, say what is wrong with the observation, and what would have to be added to make any such number mean something.

Nothing distinguishes that length from the same length multiplied by any power of Ξ, so the continuum contains a dimensionally admissible length near any value you care to name, including one that resembles a scale you already find interesting. A resemblance drawn from a continuum is not evidence.

What would have to be added is a mechanism: a dynamical reason for the exponent to take the value it takes. Weinberg's combination (ħ2H/Gc)1/3 lands near a hadronic mass scale and is famous for exactly this reason, and it remains a coincidence until something selects it.

4Three senses of the word constant

Which invariance have you actually established?

Ξ is invariant when you change the sizes of the kilogram, the metre and the second, because it has no dimensions for those changes to act on. That is a genuine result, and it is much weaker than the word constant usually suggests.

SenseWhat is being changedDoes Ξ survive?
Unit invariancemetres to feet, seconds to yearsYes, by construction, since the dimensions cancel
Constancy in timethe epoch at which you evaluate itNo: H changes with cosmic time, so Ξ does too
Frame invariancea conformal redefinition of the fieldsNo: H is not invariant under a dynamical field redefinition

The third row is the one that catches most readers. In Wetterich's description of redshift, the observation is a dimensionless ratio of wavelengths, and one frame assigns the change to an expanding scale factor while another assigns it to growing particle masses and therefore shrinking atomic rulers. Since the Bohr radius goes as ħ/(α me c), a heavier electron is a shorter ruler.

H̃ = 1Ω ( H + Ω̇/Ω )

What survives such a redefinition is a ratio of physical things: a proton mass measured against a Planck mass, which is the dimensionless gravitational strength

αG = G mp2ħ c = ( mp / mP )2

Note carefully that being invariant under the change of frame and being unchanging in time are two separate claims. αG can be the first without being the second, and in a variable-gravity cosmology that is precisely the point of it.

Someone claims to have measured a variation in the speed of light. Explain why that claim needs restating before it can be tested, and what it would have to be restated as.

0 / 40 characters

The metre is defined through c, so a change in the numerical value of c can be absorbed by a redefinition of the standards, which makes the bare claim a statement about bookkeeping rather than about the world. Any dimensioned constant is exposed to the same objection.

To be testable the claim must be restated as a variation in an operationally defined dimensionless quantity, such as the fine-structure constant α, the mass ratio me/mp, or αG. No choice of metres, feet, seconds or years can absorb a change in those.

5Now drop the ruler

From a dimensionless ratio to a torsion balance

Suppose a scalar field changes masses or couplings. A body then carries a scalar charge that depends on how its mass is assembled, and the mass of a composite body is assembled from nucleon masses, electromagnetic contributions and nuclear binding energy in proportions that differ from material to material. If the field couples to those contributions differently, two differently constituted bodies will not fall alike.

ηAB = 2 ( aA - aB )aA + aB

Universal coupling remains a perfectly respectable special case, so a varying dimensionless coupling does not by itself require composition-dependent fall. Non-universal coupling, however, makes the experiment unavoidable, which is why comparing titanium with platinum is a test of the dimensionless ledger rather than a test of heavy against light.

The final MICROSCOPE analysis compared titanium and platinum test masses in orbit and found η(Ti,Pt) = [-1.5 ± 2.3 (stat) ± 1.5 (syst)] × 10-15, consistent with universal free fall. Two elaborate assemblies of quarks, electrons, fields and binding energies were placed in orbit, and to a few parts in a thousand million million, spacetime declined to notice the difference between them.

The question the walkthrough has been building towards

Every standard you built in section 2 was made of matter from a single familiar sector, and every free-fall comparison in the table above draws its test bodies from that same sector. The cosmic scale H, which you fed into the solver without hesitation, was inferred from a gravitating inventory that is substantially larger.

We have tested with great precision whether familiar rulers fall together. We have not thereby tested every gravitating thing that helped us define the cosmic ruler.

That question has been partly asked, through torsion balances aimed at the Galactic dark-matter distribution, through lunar laser ranging, and through large-scale tracers of dark-matter motion. Those are indirect and model-dependent tests rather than drops of an isolated sample, which leaves the sharpened version of the question open: how closely has any proposed dark sector been required to share the free fall of the matter that made our rulers?

Adding H to ħ, c and G leaves a dimensionless remainder, which both dissolves the apparent menu of natural scales and exposes the cosmic hierarchy as information dimensions cannot supply. Because a dimensionless quantity built from H is still not frame-invariant, the operational content has to be carried by ratios of physical masses, of which αG is the relevant one. A field that changes αG must couple to the constituents of matter, and unless it couples to all of them identically, differently constituted bodies fall differently. Eötvös experiments therefore test whether material constructions realise that dimensionless ratio in the same way, and for laboratory matter they have found agreement to extraordinary precision. The matter that supplied the cosmic scale has not supplied a test body, so the audit is incomplete exactly where the inventory is largest.
