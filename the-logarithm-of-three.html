The Logarithm of Three

Pythagorean triples as rational points on the unit hyperbola, from Euclid's formula to relativistic velocity addition

Lee McCulloch-James · McMurmerings · October 2026

Two angles for one triangle

The 3-4-5 triangle has two acute angles whose tangents are 3/4 and 4/3, and neither angle is a rational number of degrees, nor a rational multiple of π, so the most familiar right-angled triangle in school mathematics has angles that can only be written as inverse tangents. Read on the unit hyperbola instead of the unit circle, the same triangle has two hyperbolic angles, and they are ln 2 and ln 3.

The check takes one line from the exponential definitions of cosh and sinh. With u = ln 3, so that eᵘ = 3 and e⁻ᵘ = 1/3, the hyperbolic cosine is (3 + 1/3)/2 = 5/3 and the hyperbolic sine is (3 − 1/3)/2 = 4/3, and cosh²u − sinh²u = 1 becomes 25/9 − 16/9 = 1, which is 5² − 4² = 3² with the denominator cleared. With u = ln 2 the same arithmetic gives cosh u = 5/4 and sinh u = 3/4, the same triangle with its legs exchanged.

The circular angle θ is also arc length on a unit circle. The hyperbolic angle u is not hyperbola arc length: its definition uses sector area. Below the x-axis the signed area is negative; the geometric area is its absolute value.

Euclid's formula from the definitions

Nothing about 2 and 3 was special. If eᵘ is any rational number p/q with p > q > 0, then e⁻ᵘ = q/p, and the definitions give

\[\cosh u = \frac{p^2 + q^2}{2pq},\qquad \sinh u = \frac{p^2 - q^2}{2pq},\qquad \tanh u = \frac{p^2 - q^2}{p^2 + q^2}\]

so that clearing the common denominator from cosh²u − sinh²u = 1 leaves

\[(p^2 + q^2)^2 = (p^2 - q^2)^2 + (2pq)^2\]

which is Euclid's formula for Pythagorean triples, arrived at without any geometry. When p and q have no common factor and are of opposite parity the triple (2pq, p² − q², p² + q²) is primitive; when both are odd every entry is even and halving gives a primitive triple, which is why eᵘ = 3 produced 6, 8, 10 before it produced 3, 4, 5.

The converse holds as well. A rational point (cosh u, sinh u) on the unit hyperbola has eᵘ = cosh u + sinh u rational, so the rational points on the hyperbola's right branch correspond exactly to the positive rationals, and the hyperbolic angle of a Pythagorean triangle is always the logarithm of a fraction. The logarithmic form of artanh confirms it from the other side, since applying ½ ln((1 + x)/(1 − x)) to tanh u = (p² − q²)/(p² + q²) returns ½ ln(p²/q²) = ln(p/q).

One triple on two curves

A triple a² + b² = c² can be divided through in two ways. Dividing by c² places the rational point (a/c, b/c) on the unit circle, and dividing by a² and rearranging places (c/a, b/a) on the unit hyperbola x² − y² = 1, so every Pythagorean triangle is a rational point on both curves at once, with the hypotenuse serving as the circle's radius in the first reading and as the hyperbola's x-coordinate in the second.

Both curves are parametrised rationally by a half-angle tangent. On the circle, t = tan(θ/2) gives cos θ = (1 − t²)/(1 + t²) and sin θ = 2t/(1 + t²), and on the hyperbola, t = tanh(u/2) gives

\[\cosh u = \frac{1 + t^2}{1 - t^2},\qquad \sinh u = \frac{2t}{1 - t^2}\]

the same ingredients with the roles of 1 + t² and 1 − t² exchanged, which is the difference between the elliptic and the hyperbolic in its smallest form. For the hyperbola the half-angle tangent is (p − q)/(p + q), and since tanh(u/2) = (eᵘ − 1)/(eᵘ + 1), any rational t gives a rational eᵘ and the parametrisation agrees with the one above.

What differs is the angle itself. The hyperbolic angle is a logarithm of a rational number, and two such angles add by multiplying their exponentials, which never leaves the rationals. The circular angle of the 3-4-5 triangle is arg(3 + 4i), and if it were a rational multiple of π some power of (3 + 4i)/5 would equal 1, which is impossible because 3 + 4i = (2 + i)² while 5 = (2 + i)(2 − i), and unique factorisation in the Gaussian integers forbids (2 + i)²ⁿ from ever equalling (2 + i)ⁿ(2 − i)ⁿ. On the circle the triples compose by multiplying Gaussian integers; on the hyperbola they compose by multiplying fractions, and the second operation can be done in one's head.

Adding hyperbolic angles

Because hyperbolic angles add when their exponentials multiply, the triples generated this way can be combined by mental arithmetic. Each row below takes a rational value of eᵘ and reads off the triple, listed as (a, b, c) with a the leg that serves as denominator, so that cosh u = c/a and sinh u = b/a.

eᵘ cosh u sinh u tanh u Primitive triple (a, b, c)
3/2 13/12 5/12 5/13 (12, 5, 13)
2 5/4 3/4 3/5 (4, 3, 5)
3 5/3 4/3 4/5 (3, 4, 5)
4 17/8 15/8 15/17 (8, 15, 17)
6 37/12 35/12 35/37 (12, 35, 37)
9 41/9 40/9 40/41 (9, 40, 41)

The last two rows are built from the first three. Since 6 = 2 × 3, the angle ln 6 is ln 2 + ln 3, so the two hyperbolic angles of the 3-4-5 triangle add to give the 12-35-37 triangle. Since 9 = 3², the angle ln 9 is twice ln 3, and the double-angle identity sinh 2u = 2 sinh u cosh u gives 2 · (4/3) · (5/3) = 40/9, which is the 9-40-41 triangle; likewise 4 = 2² doubles the other angle of 3-4-5 into 8-15-17, and 3/2 = 3 ÷ 2 takes the difference of the two to reach 5-12-13.

The circular angles compose as well, but less transparently. The difference of the two circular angles of 3-4-5 is the angle of the 7-24-25 triangle, found by dividing the Gaussian integers 3 + 4i and 4 + 3i, while the hyperbolic difference ln 3 − ln 2 lands on 5-12-13 by dividing 3 by 2. On the hyperbola every triangle has an address among the positive fractions, and combining two triangles is a matter of multiplying their addresses.

Rational rapidities

In special relativity the hyperbolic angle has a name, the rapidity, and the three functions in the table have physical meanings: tanh u is the speed as a fraction of c, cosh u is the Lorentz factor γ, and eᵘ is the relativistic Doppler factor √((1 + v)/(1 − v)) by which light from a receding source is stretched. A speed of 3/5 c therefore has γ = 5/4 and a Doppler factor of exactly 2, while 4/5 c has γ = 5/3 and a Doppler factor of exactly 3.

Speeds do not add in relativity, but rapidities do, and the addition formula for tanh, which follows from the exponential definitions, is the velocity-addition law:

\[\tanh(u + w) = \frac{\tanh u + \tanh w}{1 + \tanh u\,\tanh w}\]

Combining 3/5 c with 4/5 c gives (7/5) ÷ (37/25) = 35/37 c, with γ = 37/12 and Doppler factor 6, which is the 12-35-37 row of the table read as physics. The speeds whose Lorentz factor is rational are exactly the ratios b/c drawn from Pythagorean triples, because γ = 1/√(1 − v²) is rational precisely when c² − b² is a square, and the table shows that this family of speeds is closed under relativistic addition, since multiplying Doppler factors never leaves the rationals.

Exercises

  1. Find the Pythagorean triple whose hyperbolic angle is ln 5, and compare it with the row for eᵘ = 3/2.
    • Answer: cosh u = 26/10 = 13/5 and sinh u = 24/10 = 12/5, giving (5, 12, 13) with the leg 5 as denominator; the row for 3/2 is the same triangle with 12 as denominator, so ln 5 and ln(3/2) are the two hyperbolic angles of 5-12-13.
  2. Use cosh 2u = cosh²u + sinh²u and sinh 2u = 2 sinh u cosh u to double the angle ln 5, and check the triple you obtain.
    • Answer: cosh 2u = 313/25 and sinh 2u = 312/25, giving (25, 312, 313), and 25² + 312² = 97 969 = 313².
  3. A ship moving at 5/13 c launches a probe forward at 12/13 c relative to the ship. Find the probe's speed relative to the original frame and its Lorentz factor, first with the addition formula and then by multiplying Doppler factors.
    • Answer: the Doppler factors are 3/2 and 5, with product 15/2, so the speed is (225 − 4)/(225 + 4) = 221/229 c and γ = 229/60; the addition formula gives (17/13) ÷ (229/169) = 221/229 directly.
  4. Show that sinh u = 1 forces eᵘ = 1 + √2, and deduce that no Pythagorean triangle is isosceles.
    • Answer: eᵘ − e⁻ᵘ = 2 gives the quadratic (eᵘ)² − 2eᵘ − 1 = 0, whose positive root 1 + √2 is irrational, while every Pythagorean triangle has a rational eᵘ.

Coda: approaching the silver ratio

The isosceles right triangle has hyperbolic angle ln(1 + √2), the logarithm of the silver ratio, and no Pythagorean triangle can sit exactly there. They can approach it, though, and the near-isosceles triples 3-4-5, 20-21-29, 119-120-169 and 696-697-985, each with legs differing by one, have eᵘ equal to 2, 5/2, 12/5 and 29/12 when the even leg is taken as denominator, which are one more than the successive continued-fraction convergents 1, 3/2, 7/5 and 17/12 of √2.

The most familiar triangle in school geometry has hyperbolic angles ln 2 and ln 3. In the same coordinates its near-isosceles relatives are the best rational approximations to a metallic mean, and their velocities, read as fractions of c, form a family closed under relativistic addition. Each of these facts uses only the hyperbolic definitions, one identity and the logarithmic form of artanh, together with the oldest formula in number theory.
