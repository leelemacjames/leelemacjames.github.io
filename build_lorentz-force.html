A staged problem — electromagnetism & frames of reference

What Kind of Force Depends on Motion?

A charge beside a magnet, a question that sounds almost like a category error, and a law that only makes sense once several stranger ones have been asked first.

By this point, every force met so far has depended on where things are: gravity on mass and separation, a spring on how far it is stretched, the Coulomb force on charge and distance. None of those cares how fast anything is moving. Then a force shows up that depends on velocity, and the honest first reaction is not to calculate it, but to ask how a force could possibly depend on speed at all.

Deliberately withheld, for now
  • no formula for the force
  • no assumption that force is the same for every observer
  • no claim that electricity and magnetism are separate ingredients
Questions worth sitting with
  • What if I stand beside the particle?
  • What if I move along with it?
  • Can one observer see a force while another does not?
  • Is force observer-dependent?
  • Does magnetism exist because of relativity?

A charge, still

Place a stationary charge beside a magnet and nothing happens; there is no force to measure at all. The tool below starts exactly there, with the velocity arrow collapsed to nothing.

Drag the tip of the brass arrow away from the centre.

02

Set it moving

Give the charge some velocity, in almost any direction, by dragging the tip of the brass arrow away from the centre. A force appears where a moment ago there was none, and nothing about the charge itself, its size, its charge, its mass, has changed at all.

03

Faster still

Drag the tip further out, keeping roughly the same direction. The force grows. Whatever is generating it scales with speed in a way none of the earlier forces in the course ever did.

04

Parallel to the field

Now swing the arrow around until it lies along the teal field arrow, pointing the same way. However far out the tip is dragged, the force collapses back to nothing. Motion alone was not enough; the direction of that motion, relative to the field, turns out to matter just as much as the speed.

05

Perpendicular to the field

Swing the arrow around until it stands at a right angle to the field instead. For a given speed, this is where the force is largest, the exact opposite of stage four.

06

Only afterwards, reveal the law

Five stages of dragging have already established that the force depends on speed, on the angle between velocity and field, vanishes when they are parallel, and peaks when they are perpendicular. That is enough evidence to make the law below feel inevitable rather than arbitrary.

Reveal the law
F = qv × B
|F| = qvB sinθ

The cross product is exactly the shape the tool has been demonstrating by hand: force proportional to speed, proportional to the sine of the angle between velocity and field, zero when they are parallel, maximal when they are perpendicular, and always at right angles to the velocity itself, which is why it appeared here as something happening in front of or behind the page rather than as an arrow drawn in the plane.

Whose force is it?

Can one observer see a force while another does not?Yes.

Does magnetism exist because of relativity?In a real sense, yes.

An observer who moves alongside the charge, matching its velocity exactly, sees a charge at rest beside a magnet, and stage one already established that a stationary charge feels no magnetic force whatsoever. The two observers disagree about whether a force is acting on the same charge at the same instant, and reconciling that disagreement is most of what special relativity has to say about electromagnetism: electric and magnetic fields turn out not to be two separate ingredients of nature, but a single electromagnetic field, sliced differently by observers moving at different velocities through it.
