Human Reporting Standards · Volume I

Provisions, Impairment and Contingent Lives

Being the requirements for the recognition, measurement and disclosure of obligations, losses and unled alternatives in the accounts of a single person.

StatusIssued. Mandatory.
EffectiveImmediately. No comparative period exists for which the entity is not already responsible.
Early adoptionEncouraged. On the evidence, rare.

Preface

These Standards exist because the absence of common rules is not freedom, it is incomparability, and because a preparer who is also the only reader will drift without noticing. The Board makes no claim to know how any entity should conduct itself; it determines only which statements about that conduct are permissible, and it does so in advance of the periods in which the entity will be tempted.

Every requirement below is a fossil of a specific fraud, observed repeatedly, and written down afterwards.

Extract from the Conceptual Framework

CF.1Scope. These Standards govern provenance and not providence. They prescribe how an entity shall establish whence its account arose and make no determination concerning whither the entity ought to proceed. An inability to specify a destination does not, of itself, rebut the going-concern assumption.
CF.2Users. A life has no external users. The accounts shall nevertheless be prepared as though there existed a user who is neither the preparer nor a person who loves the preparer.
CF.7Neutrality. Prudence is the exercise of caution under uncertainty. Prudence does not permit the deliberate overstatement of liabilities or the understatement of assets. Systematic pessimism is a misstatement, and it is a misstatement in the direction nobody audits for.
CF.11Materiality. An item is material if its omission could reasonably be expected to influence the decision of a user. It follows that most of what the entity agonises over is immaterial, and that a small number of items it has never mentioned are not.
CF.14Faithful representation. An account may be complete, neutral and free from error and still be false, where the entity has selected a unit of account at which everything it possesses appears negligible. Scale is a policy choice and shall be disclosed.

The Standards

HRS 37

Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets

after IAS 37 · the standard written because provisions were being released to order

37.14A provision shall be recognised only where all three conditions are met.
Recognition criteria
  1. a present obligation arising from a past event;
  2. probable outflow of resources; and
  3. a reliable estimate of the amount.
37.15An uncertain aging future does not satisfy the third condition. Where no reliable estimate can be made, the entity shall disclose a contingent liability in the notes and recognise nothing. Disclosure costs nothing in the current period. Recognition costs the current period everything.
37.19Contingent assets shall not be recognised.
37.20The life the entity did not lead is a contingent asset. It shall not be recognised, shall not be carried forward, and shall not appear in the primary statements. Where its inflow becomes virtually certain the item ceases to be contingent, at which point it is an ordinary asset and this paragraph does not apply to it.
37.21Opportunity cost. Only the best alternative actually foregone may be considered for decision-making purposes. The entity shall not aggregate the most attractive features of mutually exclusive lives and recognise the total as a loss. No such aggregate was ever available to it.
37.23Where the effect of the time value of waiting is material, a provision shall be measured at present value. The unwinding of that discount is a cost of each period of waiting and shall be expensed as incurred. It shall not be netted against the eventual benefit, and it shall not be described as patience.
37.31Onerous arrangements. Where the unavoidable costs of meeting an obligation exceed the benefits expected from it, the entity shall recognise the shortfall immediately. An arrangement continued out of duty is an onerous contract and is disclosable as such.
37.40A provision shall not be recognised in one period for the purpose of improving the appearance of a later one. The practice of writing off a decade so that whatever follows may be reported as recovery is prohibited.

HRS 27

Separate Lives and Consolidated Comparators

after IAS 27 and IFRS 10 · the standard governing fear of missing out

27.4An entity does not control the persons it follows and shall not consolidate their accounts. Observation, admiration and sustained resentment do not constitute control.
27.8The entity shall not combine the promotion of one comparator, the marriage of a second, the travels of a third and the apparent serenity of a fourth, and present the resulting aggregate as a life it has failed to lead.
27.9Such a comparator has never existed. A shortfall measured against it does not meet the definition of a liability.
27.10The Board considered whether repeated exposure creates a constructive obligation to keep pace. It concluded that it does not.

HRS 36

Impairment of Assets

after IAS 36 · why the write-down lands in one period and belongs to twenty

36.9Assets with indefinite useful lives shall be tested for impairment annually, irrespective of whether any indication of impairment exists. The exceptional life is such an asset. The requirement applies precisely because no entity would otherwise look at it.
36.12Indications include: an expansion of the comparison class; the revision of the plan for a third time; external evidence obtained without being sought; and the arrival of a birthday that the entity had earlier used as a deadline.
36.60An impairment loss shall be recognised in full in the period in which it is identified, notwithstanding that the decline in value occurred across many. Allocation of the loss to the prior periods to which it economically relates is prohibited.
36.61No cash leaves the entity on impairment. The entity will nevertheless experience the adjustment as a departure. This is expected and does not indicate an error in measurement.
36.117Reversal is permitted where the estimates used to determine recoverable amount have changed. It is not permitted for internally generated goodwill, which may not be reversed for the reason given at HRS 38.48, namely that it was never permitted to be recognised.

HRS 38

Intangible Assets

after IAS 38 · potential is not an asset

38.48Internally generated goodwill shall not be recognised. The entity's own estimate of what it could have been does not arise from a past transaction and is not capable of reliable measurement. It is not an asset. It has never been an asset.
38.51Goodwill generated by others and conferred upon the entity in childhood is likewise not recognisable by the entity, although the Board acknowledges it will be carried anyway, and that its carrying value is rarely reviewed.
38.57Development expenditure may be capitalised only where technical feasibility, intention to complete, ability to use, and probable future benefit can each be demonstrated. Otherwise it is expensed as incurred. A practice not yet practised is an expense.

HRS 8

Accounting Policies, Changes in Estimates, and Errors

after IAS 8 · the standard governing regret

8.5A change in estimate arises from new information. An error arises from a failure to use information that was available and could reasonably have been obtained at the time.
8.32A change in estimate shall be applied prospectively only. The entity shall not restate prior periods on the grounds that it now knows more.
8.36A decision that was reasonable on the information then available is not an error, however it turned out. Retrospective restatement in such cases is prohibited and, where performed nightly, shall be disclosed as a change in accounting policy.
8.37Where the information was available, the item is an error, and retrospective restatement is mandatory. The entity does not have the option of treating it as an estimate. The Board notes that entities in this position display a marked preference for cosmological units of account, and refers them to CF.14.

HRS 24

Related Party Disclosures

after IAS 24 · terms obtained from people who love you are not evidence of value

24.9A related party is any person from whom terms cannot be assumed to be at arm's length. The class includes those who love the entity, those who depend upon it, those who employ it, and those it has harmed.
24.18Transactions with related parties shall be disclosed whether or not consideration passed. The absence of a price is itself a disclosable term.
24.23A statement that terms were equivalent to those prevailing in an arm's length transaction shall be made only where the entity can substantiate it. Most such statements are made where it cannot.

HRS 10

Events after the Reporting Period

after IAS 10 · which later discoveries reach backwards

10.8An adjusting event provides evidence of a condition that existed at the reporting date. The prior period shall be restated.
10.10A non-adjusting event reflects a condition arising afterwards. The prior period shall not be restated; the event shall be disclosed where material.
10.14Learning that one was deceived is adjusting. Learning that one was left is ordinarily not. The distinction turns entirely on whether the condition already obtained, and the entity's distress is not evidence either way.

HRS 16

Property, Plant and Equipment

after IAS 16 · the body, held at cost less accumulated depreciation

16.6Depreciation is the systematic allocation of the depreciable amount over the useful life. It is not a loss event. It is expected, it is recognised in every period including the good ones, and an entity that recognises it only when distressed has been misreporting throughout.
16.13Parts requiring replacement at regular intervals shall be capitalised separately and depreciated over the interval. Sleep, teeth, strength and the friendships requiring maintenance fall within this paragraph. Expenditure on them is not consumption.
16.51Useful life and residual value shall be reviewed at least at each reporting date. Revision is a change in estimate under HRS 8 and is never an error. Entities revise downward without evidence far more often than they revise upward with it.

HRS 19

Employee Benefits and Salary Sacrifice

after IAS 19 · the future self is seldom consulted

19.8The present and future selves are reporting periods of the same entity. Transfers between them shall not be presented as gifts.
19.9An arrangement described by payroll as salary sacrifice shall be accounted for according to its substance. Where present salary is exchanged for probable future benefit and tax relief, no moral credit shall be recognised.
19.10The terminology selected by the payroll department does not determine whether an act constitutes renunciation. The Board has found no basis for treating tax efficiency as asceticism.
19.11Gratification deferred beyond twelve months shall be classified as non-current. Non-current does not mean imaginary.
19.12Expected gratification shall be reassessed where the preferences of the future self change. Such revision is a change in estimate under HRS 8 and does not constitute ingratitude.
19.13The future self is not a separate entity, although the Board acknowledges that it is rarely afforded representation during negotiations.

HRS 15

Revenue from Contracts

after IFRS 15 · when an experience is earned

15.31Revenue is recognised when a performance obligation is satisfied, that is, when the experience is delivered.
15.38An experience is delivered when the entity has it. It is not delivered when it is recorded, described, or observed by a third party. Recognition on the basis of the observations of others is prohibited, and an entity applying that basis will report revenue it did not earn while omitting revenue it did.
15.44Consideration received in advance of performance is a liability, not revenue. Anticipation is deferred income.

HRS 12

Deferred Items and Unused Losses

after IAS 12 · the carryforward is a solvency claim

12.34A deferred asset arising from unused losses shall be recognised only to the extent that future profit against which to set them is probable. A loss carryforward is worthless to an entity that will not earn again.
12.56The asset shall be reassessed at each reporting date. Its continued recognition constitutes an assertion that there will be periods yet to come, and the entity shall be taken to have made that assertion whether or not it would repeat it aloud.

HRS 1

Presentation and Going Concern

after IAS 1 · the uncertainty is not the misstatement

1.25Accounts are prepared on a going concern basis unless the entity intends or has no realistic alternative but to cease. Where a material uncertainty exists, it shall be disclosed. The failure to disclose is the misstatement; the uncertainty is not.
1.45Presentation shall be consistent between periods. Where the same period is reclassified as a triumph in one telling and as waste in the next, the reason for the reclassification shall be disclosed, together with the audience before whom each was presented.

Basis for conclusions

The Board considered whether HRS 37.20 imposes hardship. Several respondents observed that the prohibition on recognising the unled life removes the only asset some entities believe themselves to hold. The Board accepts this and declines to amend. An asset which cannot be measured, transferred, spent or realised performs no function in the accounts, and its removal does not diminish the entity.

Dissenting opinion — one member

The dissenting member would permit retrospective restatement in cases of moral error, on the ground that an entity which has caused harm cannot be confined to prospective application while the consequences of its conduct are borne continuously by another party.

The Board rejects the proposed exception as unnecessary. Where the information was available at the time, the item is already an error under HRS 8.37, restatement is already mandatory, and no exception is required in order to compel it. The Board observes that the member's difficulty is unlikely to be one of standard-setting.

The Board reminds preparers that a faithfully represented life may remain unsatisfactory. This is a condition of the underlying entity and not a reporting error.
